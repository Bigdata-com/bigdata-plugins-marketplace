# Packaging, 28 October 2026

## Why the skills were not winning

On 28 October a blind reader compared the earnings-preview report written with the skill (4,107 words, 11 of 12 assertions) against the one written without it (2,479 words, 9 of 11) and preferred the one without. The with-skill report carried "derived (expectations formula 1)", "software primer, backlog duration", a paragraph of base rates that named the book, Mauboussin's drift tables, thirty basis labels and an opening of five bold run-in headings copied from the skill's own list. Across the fifteen with-skill reports from 26 and 27 October, ten named a library file, formula, primer or source book on the page; none of the ten no-skill reports did.

Each piece of residue traced to an instruction. Rule 7 of this proposal asked for a base rate next to every growth claim "labelled with the sample and period". The library was written as a verified literature digest, 23 to 97 attributions per method file, with page references and "Not in the sources" and "(verify)" notes meant for the maintainer. The output standard said every claim needs a source or a basis word and a derived figure must "name the formula once". The skill bodies addressed the library by number ("formula 4", "tables 1 and 2"), and the method files carried their own numbered procedures, which the agent executed as report sections. Given a number whose only source was a book, a rule that unsourced numbers stay out, and an instruction to quote N and sample, the agent cited the book. Given a rule to name the formula and a body that names formulas by number, it wrote "expectations formula 1".

The deeper problem was the test. The assertions were presence checks, most derived from the skill's own list, so three rounds of tuning made the skill longer and more compliant while the note got worse for the reader.

## The standard

The reader wants a high-quality, actionable write-up about the company and the question. Everything that exists to produce it stays out of it. The note reads as the work of an analyst who knows the method. One note, one bar: a portfolio manager and an investor-relations team get the same note and skip what they do not need.

## Three layers

- **Provenance, for the maintainer, never shipped.** Sources, editions, pages, exhibits, what the sources do not cover, verification status. They live in `method-specs/` beside this proposal. The build does not copy them.
- **The library, shipped, the agent's working knowledge.** Written as a firm's internal manual: no author, paper, book or bank named; formulas and tables named by what they compute, never numbered; a historical figure carries only its population and period; techniques, not procedures; limits as conditions the analyst can act on.
- **The output rules, what the reader sees.** A fact from a document carries a linked source and date. An assumption a conclusion rests on is stated once, in plain words, where it matters. Nothing else is labelled. Methods, formulas, historical statistics and the skill's files are never cited or named. Sub-headings state findings, not steps. The note never describes its own making.

Rules 5, 7 and 11 of section 3 were revised to match. The basis words, the Basis column, the base-rate-next-to-every-claim rule and the "(verify)" convention are retired.

## What a body is now

Directions with their reasons, each conditional where it is not always needed, pointing at a method file by the section's content. No artefact list, no eval anecdotes, no label vocabulary, no reader re-framing. Where a note uses an enterprise value, the balance sheet appears once from the filing and every later figure uses it. Where a note builds cases, they partition the outcomes with one cut-off per metric, the probabilities start from the company's own record, and the weighted result reconciles with the table. The checks before sending are things the agent can verify on the page.

## Evidence

Micro-test of the output rules (five single-shot runs per arm on the same working notes): the old rules produced 13 to 18 basis labels per 440-word section and a method or source name in two of five; the new rules produced none in five of five.

What the judges credited: the six-quarter guidance record with the usual beat applied, the price-implied segment growth with a stated sensitivity, the balance sheet read from the 10-Q (which corrected the stale net-cash figure the no-skill note used), numeric thresholds in the release checklist, call questions, scenario weights that reconcile. What the no-skill note did better and the skill now asks for: the same-week read-across from peers reporting first, accounting and policy items that move the stock, the tell in management's words beside each case, a title that carries the setup.

Anthropic's own `equity-research:earnings-preview` skill (416 words: consensus by web search, a sector metrics list, a bull/base/bear table, a catalyst checklist, the options-implied move) was read for gaps. Four lines were borrowed, listed above; the rest it has, ours has in more depth, and it carries a rating and price target, which ours never does.

## Applied across the plugin

The same day: the output standard and the fourteen method files rewritten to the library layer; the twelve sector primers stripped of sources; every skill body revised to the body rules above, pointing at method files by content; the checker gained a leak lint that fails any body pointing at the library by number or any shipped reference naming a source, an author note or a basis label. ## Five more skills, the same evening

Five skills ran on their spare live prompts, one with-skill run against one no-skill run, blind Fable judges with the brutal brief. The three investor-voice prompts went to the skill, each "not close on rigour": the Meta stock move, the Netflix recap, the Deckers valuation. The two corporate-voice prompts went to the no-skill note, both "close": the JetBlue brief for a competitor and US refining for a refiner. The losses had one cause a level below the residue: the bodies still mandated analytical pieces (an event table, the five forces, the profit pool, margin fade, leader-by-leader implied growth, base-rate sentences) whatever the question, so the reader's own parts were skipped or thinned. Every body then got a second pass: the user's parts first and in order, every piece conditional on the question reaching it, no table named by its columns, classifications as sentences, the inputs behind any value stated once. The two lost prompts were rerun on the revised bodies against the same no-skill notes: structure fixed and no residue left, but both still lost narrowly, because the reader's last part ("what it means for us", "where do we sit") got less depth than the valuation. The output standard now says the last part gets the same depth and arithmetic as the first. Marko then pointed at the tables: the with-skill notes averaged 7.3 columns at the widest table and header rows of 113 characters against 5.8 and 85 without the skill, because the output standard pushed unit, period and assumptions into headings, the library's dense reference tables were imitated, and bodies asked for comparisons on identical rows. A table recipe went into the output standard (at most four columns, short headings, qualifiers in one line under the table, one measure per cell, narrative cells for case comparisons); the refining note rerun on it had three readable tables and won its blind comparison. The corporate reader's own positioning is still the thinner part and is where the next round starts.

## Still open

The pass rule (two prompts per skill, blind Fable judge, win both within budget) has run on six skills; earnings preview passed six of six pairs, three investor prompts passed, two corporate prompts lost narrowly and are the next round's start. Tokens with the skill run about 1.5 times the no-skill run. The company tearsheet returned server errors in two of eight runs on 28 October.
