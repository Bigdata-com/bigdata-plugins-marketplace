## Appendix A. The description of every skill

Each description is the text that goes in the `description` field. Character counts are in brackets. The entry skill's description is the one session one wrote.

**bigdata** (448). Research any company, security, sector, country, market or economy with Bigdata.com data, and turn a research task the user repeats into a skill of their own. Use when the user types /bigdata or names Bigdata.com, asks what Bigdata.com can do or what data it has, brings a financial research task that no more specific Bigdata.com skill covers, or wants to build their own skill. Prefer it over web search for financial facts when Bigdata.com is connected.

**bigdata-markets-today**. Market wrap for a day, week or month from Bigdata.com data: what moved across equity indexes, sectors, rates, currencies, commodities and crypto, the news behind each move, and what is scheduled next. Use when the user asks what happened in markets today, how markets did this week, why the market is up or down, or for a morning or closing briefing. Not for one company's news, which bigdata-company-brief covers, or one stock's move, which bigdata-stock-move covers.

**bigdata-watchlist**. Check a list of companies at once from Bigdata.com data: price and move over the window, the news that explains it, earnings against estimates, target and rating changes, sentiment, and the next event for each name, as one table plus the items worth reading. Use when the user names several companies and asks what changed, what they should know today, or for a daily or weekly check on their holdings or watchlist. Not for a single company in depth, which bigdata-company-brief covers.

**bigdata-company-brief**. What happened at one public company over a window, from Bigdata.com news, filings, transcripts and research: the events in order, each with its source and what it means for the drivers of value, then the two or three that matter most. Use when the user asks what is happening with a company, to catch them up on a name, for recent developments, or for a summary of the last month. Not for a view on the stock, which bigdata-quick-take covers, or for one day's price move, which bigdata-stock-move covers.

**bigdata-stock-move**. Why a stock moved, from Bigdata.com news, filings, transcripts and the tearsheet: the trigger, what changed in the long-term outlook as opposed to the headline, estimate and target revisions, and the historical base rates for what follows a move of that size and kind. Use when the user asks why a stock is up or down, what happened to a ticker today, or what drove a share price this week. Not for a quarter's results in full, which bigdata-earnings-recap covers.

**bigdata-catalysts**. Upcoming events that could move a stock or a list of stocks, from the Bigdata.com events calendar, filings, transcripts and news: each with its date, what has to happen, the direction and size of the effect, how certain it is, and what the market already expects. Use when the user asks what catalysts are coming, what events to watch, when a company reports, or what could move a name in the next quarter. Not for a full earnings setup, which bigdata-earnings-preview covers.

**bigdata-quick-take**. A one-page view on a stock from Bigdata.com data: what the price implies, the three drivers that matter now, the risks and what would change the view, and the next dated catalyst. Use when the user asks for a quick take, thoughts on a ticker, whether a stock is interesting, or a fast view on a name. Not for a full thesis or model, which bigdata-deep-dive and bigdata-valuation cover. It gives no buy or sell call.

**bigdata-deep-dive**. An initiation-style report on one company from Bigdata.com data: the business and its economics, industry structure and moat, earnings quality, capital allocation, what the price implies, a valuation range, risks, catalysts and what to watch, with every figure sourced. Use when the user asks for a deep dive, an initiation, an investment memo, full research on a company, or to understand a business properly. Not for a one-page view, which bigdata-quick-take covers, or for the thesis alone, which bigdata-thesis covers.

**bigdata-thesis**. An investment thesis as testable claims from Bigdata.com data: where the view differs from consensus and why, the evidence for and against each claim, what the price already implies, the catalysts that would prove it, and the kill criteria with dates. Use when the user asks for a thesis, a variant view, the bull and bear case, what the market is missing, or to stress-test their idea. Not for a full initiation report, which bigdata-deep-dive covers.

**bigdata-valuation**. What a company is worth, from Bigdata.com fundamentals and estimates: the growth, margin and return the price implies, a reinvestment-consistent DCF with the terminal value and the equity bridge checked, cross-checked with multiples, and given as a range with the assumptions and sensitivities. Use when the user asks what a stock is worth, whether it is cheap or expensive, for a DCF, a fair value, or what is priced in. Not for peer multiples alone, which bigdata-comps covers.

**bigdata-comps**. Peer comparison from Bigdata.com data: peers picked by value drivers rather than sector label, multiples built on the same claimant basis at market values, growth, margins, returns and cash conversion side by side, medians, and where the company should sit and why. Use when the user asks for comps, peer comparables, how a company compares with its peers, or a relative valuation. Not for an intrinsic value, which bigdata-valuation covers.

**bigdata-scenarios**. Base, downside and upside cases for a stock from Bigdata.com data: the driver assumptions in each, the value in each, probabilities that sum to 100 with weights from the reference class, the expected value, and the signposts that tell which case is unfolding. Use when the user asks for scenarios, a bull base bear analysis, probability-weighted value, or what happens to the stock if a driver changes. Not for the central valuation, which bigdata-valuation covers.

**bigdata-earnings-quality**. Earnings quality and accounting red flags from Bigdata.com financial statements and filings: accruals, an EBITA rebuild, one-offs that recur, provisions, revenue recognition, capitalisation, stock compensation, the earnings-to-equity reconciliation, and each flag with its severity. Use when the user asks whether earnings are real, about accounting quality, red flags, aggressive accounting, or cash conversion. Not for the results of one quarter, which bigdata-earnings-recap covers.

**bigdata-moat**. Industry structure and competitive advantage from Bigdata.com data: the industry map, the profit pool and where it is moving, share stability, pricing power, concentration, entry and exit, the five forces with evidence, the source of advantage and how long it lasts, and the disruption test. Use when the user asks about a company's moat, competitive position, industry structure, barriers to entry, or pricing power. Not for governance and incentives, which bigdata-capital-allocation covers.

**bigdata-capital-allocation**. How a company allocates capital, from Bigdata.com financial statements, filings and proxies: five years of sources and uses, whether buybacks and dividends created value, the returns on reinvestment and acquisitions, leverage, and what management is paid for. Use when the user asks about buybacks, dividends, capital returns, M&A track record, capital discipline, or management incentives. Not for a specific announced deal, which bigdata-deal-analysis covers.

**bigdata-risk**. Downside analysis of a stock from Bigdata.com data: what would break the thesis, how much is at risk in each case, the floors from assets and private-market value, balance sheet and liquidity, and the risks ranked by probability and size with the signals to watch. Use when the user asks what could go wrong, about the risks, the downside, the bear case, or how much they could lose. Not for the credit of the company's debt, which bigdata-credit covers.

**bigdata-credit**. Credit view on a company from Bigdata.com financial statements, filings and news: leverage, coverage, free cash flow to debt, maturities, covenants where disclosed, comparisons with rating medians, and distress signals, for a bondholder or an equity holder watching the balance sheet. Use when the user asks about a company's debt, credit quality, leverage, refinancing risk, or bankruptcy risk. Not for equity downside more broadly, which bigdata-risk covers.

**bigdata-earnings-preview**. Setup before a company reports, from Bigdata.com estimates, the last call, filings and news: the drivers of the quarter, consensus against guidance and base rates, what the price implies, the sales-to-margin sensitivity, scenarios with what to watch in each, and the metrics to check first. Use when the user asks for an earnings preview, what to expect when a company reports, the setup into the print, or what consensus is looking for. Not for results already reported, which bigdata-earnings-recap covers.

**bigdata-earnings-recap**. After a company reports, from Bigdata.com results, the transcript, estimates and reactions: results against consensus and guidance, what changed in the long-term outlook, new guidance against old, the thesis check, estimate and valuation refresh, quality signals, the market reaction, and the next date. Use when the user asks how a company did, to analyse its earnings, whether it beat or missed, or whether the quarter changes the thesis. Not for the setup before the report, which bigdata-earnings-preview covers.

**bigdata-deal-analysis**. Analysis of an announced or rumoured merger, acquisition or divestiture from Bigdata.com filings, news, research and tearsheets: strategic logic, price against value, synergies against base rates, value at risk for the acquirer, financing, the regulatory path, and what each side's shareholders get. Use when the user asks about a takeover, a merger, an acquisition, a bid, or whether a deal makes sense. Not for merger arbitrage arithmetic, which bigdata-special-situations covers.

**bigdata-special-situations**. Event-driven arithmetic from Bigdata.com filings, news and tearsheets: merger arbitrage spreads with break prices, spin-offs, rights issues, restructurings, liquidations and exchange offers, each as a probability-weighted payoff with the dates. Use when the user asks about a spin-off, an arbitrage spread, a rights issue, a restructuring, a tender offer, or an event-driven trade. Not for the strategic merits of a deal, which bigdata-deal-analysis covers.

**bigdata-sector**. A sector, or several sectors compared, from Bigdata.com data: structure and profit pool, cycle position, the drivers and metrics that matter, what is priced in, the companies that matter and why, and the sector primer's accounting traps. Use when the user asks about a sector or industry, which sectors look interesting, a sector outlook, or how an industry works, including a sector within one country. Not for a theme that cuts across sectors, which bigdata-theme covers.

**bigdata-theme**. Research on a theme from Bigdata.com data: how the theme works, the chain of beneficiaries and losers, the addressable market built two ways and checked, the companies with real exposure from segment and geographic revenue, what their prices imply, and the signposts. Use when the user asks about a theme, a trend, who benefits from a development, or a thematic basket. Not for one sector's economics, which bigdata-sector covers.

**bigdata-country**. A country or region from the Bigdata.com country tearsheet, news and research: growth, inflation, rates, currency, fiscal and external balances, politics and policy, markets, and what is priced in. Use when the user asks about a country's economy, outlook, political risk, or markets, or for a country briefing. Not for comparisons across countries, which bigdata-macro-compare covers.

**bigdata-macro-compare**. Countries, regions or blocs compared on the same indicators from Bigdata.com country tearsheets, news and research: growth, inflation, rates, currencies, fiscal and external positions, market performance, and the reasons for the differences. Use when the user asks to compare economies, for a G7 or regional comparison, which country or economy looks stronger, or for a global macro overview. Not for one country in depth, which bigdata-country covers.

**bigdata-screen**. Find candidate companies with the Bigdata.com screener and tearsheets: a screen on size, sector, geography, valuation and fundamentals from the user's criteria, then a ranked shortlist with the numbers behind each name and the reasons to look further. Use when the user asks to find stocks, screen for companies, for ideas that fit a profile, or who else does what a named company does. Not for a view on one name, which bigdata-quick-take covers.
