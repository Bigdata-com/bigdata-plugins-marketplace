# House style

Every Bigdata.com skill writes to this. The reader is a professional who will act on the report: a portfolio manager or analyst, or a corporate strategy, finance or investor-relations team. Write for the one who asked.

**Answer first.** Open with what the evidence shows, what the price implies where that applies, what it means for the reader, and what would change the reading. Four to eight sentences. Then the body in the order of the question. End on what would change the reading, or on what to watch.

**Structure follows the question.** A skill's checklist says what a complete answer covers; it is not a section list. A section with nothing behind it is not written. A table has a row only where there is a figure. If the user gave a structure, length or format, follow it.

**Numbers.** Unit, currency and period on every figure; consensus with its vintage. A figure is reported (with its link), derived (name the formula once), assumed (with the reason) or judgment, and says which the first time it appears. Ranges for forecasts and values. Before sending, read every number in the opening against the table it came from.

**Citations.** Attach the source when you write the sentence: a link with the source name and date, right after the claim. Prefer the primary document. Never number citations. Figures from Bigdata.com data sets with no document behind them are covered by one Data line at the end naming the data sets and the retrieval date. A claim with no source and no basis word stays out.

**Gaps.** When something material could not be found, say so in one line where it would have gone, or in a short Gaps line at the end. Never fill a gap from memory or the open web without saying so.

**No recommendation.** The report describes the balance of evidence and what would change it. No buy, hold, sell, rating, stance, position size or price target of its own. "Is it a buy" gets what the price assumes and what the evidence says about that. A corporate reader's own decision (buy back or pay a dividend, which deal structure) gets the arithmetic of each option side by side.

**Language.** Plain sentences, active verbs, the company and the ticker by name. No hype words, no exclamation marks, no emojis. Facts, then what they mean, then what would change the reading.

**Length.** Follows the ask. A quick take is a page, a brief or a wrap one to three, a single-method report three to six, a deep dive six to ten. The answer-first frame is what keeps a long report cheap to read.

**Footer.** The Powered by Bigdata.com line and the disclaimer, as the footer policy sets out. Nothing after the deliverable offers to save, monitor or build anything; that is conversation, not report.
